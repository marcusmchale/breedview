# Your account and personal data

Your profile shows what BreedGraph holds about you, and is where you change it: your account, the person record that
credits you for your work, and your teams. You can unlink or erase the person record, and delete your account, at any
time.

> [!WHO]
> Anyone with an account, for their own account and the person record linked to it.

## Before you start

You need an account: here, Nina's, from [Joining BreedGraph](/guide/joining), with her person record linked to it.

## Steps

1. **Your profile shows what BreedGraph holds about you.** Your account, the person record linked to it, and the teams
   you belong to, with the access you hold in each. {#profile}
2. **Edit account changes your account's details.** Your username, full name, email address and password. {#account}
3. **Your person record is how you are credited.** Edit name and institution corrects it; you can also connect your
   ORCID iD to it. If you add to the ontology, you can choose to be named among its contributors, which shows your
   record's name, institution and ORCID iD to anyone, with the versions your work is in. {#person}
4. **Unlinking keeps the record; erasing removes you from it.** Unlink leaves the record in place, to be claimed again.
   Erase removes its name, institution, teams and ORCID iD, keeping only an anonymous id, so research data stays
   attributed without identifying you. {#erase}
5. **Delete my account ends your login.** Your username, full name, email address, password and team memberships are
   removed, and your username is free to register again. What you contributed stays attributed to an anonymous id.
   {#delete}

## Who sees what

> [!SEES]
> Your account's details are shown to you. Your person record is shown to signed-in users it is released to, and once
> erased, only as an anonymous id.

> [!DATA]
> Erasing a person record and deleting an account cannot be undone. Both are logged, by record and account number,
> outside the database, so that they are applied again if a backup is ever restored. If you have no account, or cannot
> reach a record yourself, ask the University, as the privacy notice describes: once they have confirmed who you are,
> they can erase the record for you.
