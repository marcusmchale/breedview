# Exploring a study's data

The table of a study's records can show them in the shape you need: each record on a row of its own, or each unit's
records of a day, a month or a year together, with the factors that apply to them and the details of each unit beside
them. This chapter uses the public 2024 study, which anyone can open.

> [!WHO]
> Anyone. Visitors see the records teams have made public; signed in, you also see those your teams can read.

## Before you start

Accept the Terms of Use, and the study's licence, as in [Looking around as a visitor](/guide/looking-around).

![Exploring a study's data](exploring-data.mp4)

## Steps

1. **View datasets opens a study's records.** On the Programs page, open the study's Datasets, then View datasets.
   Choose the variables and the factor, then Load Table. {#open}
2. **Same year puts each plot on one row.** Rows chooses which records share a row: each on its own, or a unit's
   records of the same time, day, month or year. Each value keeps its own date: point at one to see it. {#rows}
3. **Factors pass down the unit graph.** The fungicide programme was recorded once, on the trial area. Each plot
   inherits it from the unit above it, so it shows on every plot's row, as applied. Point at it to see where it comes
   from. {#factors}
4. **Other contextual details can be included in the table, such as the germplasm and location.** Columns lists
   the details a row can show. {#columns}

## Who sees what

> [!SEES]
> The table shows only the records you can read: a visitor sees those released publicly. A unit hidden from you shows
> as a placeholder, so that the units below it keep their place.

> [!DATA]
> Visitors see no personal data here. Signed in, a record's 🔒 shows the team that controls it, and who contributed to
> the dataset.

## Try it on the demo site

Open the 2024 study in Long Meadow and View datasets. Try each choice of Rows, and the columns a row can show.
