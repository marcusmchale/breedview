# Describing what you measure

Everything BreedGraph records is described by its ontology: the subjects observed, such as a plant or a plot, what is
observed of them, how, and on what scale. Next season's trials will measure the flag leaf, which the ontology has no
words for yet. This chapter adds them, as drafts, and commits them as a new version of the ontology, ready for the
forms.

> [!WHO]
> Those with an ontology role. Contributors draft new entries; editors also change entries and commit versions; admins
> also grant the roles, as in [Looking after the ontology](/guide/looking-after-the-ontology). Here, Dara Data, the
> ontology's admin.

## Before you start

Look for an entry before making one: the ontology's search finds entries by name, and an entry that exists, such as the
Centimetres scale here, is reused rather than made again.

## Steps

1. **Create Entries adds a draft trait.** A trait is what is observed: Dara creates Flag leaf length, with what it means,
   and the subject it describes, a Plant. {#trait}
2. **An observation method says how it is observed.** Ruler on the flag leaf, a measurement, with how it is done.
   {#method}
3. **A variable is a trait observed by a method on a scale.** Flag leaf length (cm) brings the new trait and method
   together with the existing Centimetres scale. Datasets record variables, so this is what the forms will offer.
   {#variable}
4. **Drafts are kept out of the forms.** Showing the draft phase alone picks out the three new entries. Until a commit
   makes them part of a version, nothing can be recorded with them, as a draft may yet be changed or removed. {#drafts}
5. **Commit Changes makes the drafts a new version.** A minor version, as it adds entries, with a comment saying why.
   The ontology's licence and copyright go with the version, found by searching; they must be public, as the ontology
   is shared. {#commit}
6. **View History lists the versions.** The new version 2.2.0 heads the list, with its comment. Display shows the
   ontology as any version had it. {#history}

## Who sees what

> [!SEES]
> The ontology is shared by everyone who uses BreedGraph. Its latest version is public, so visitors see it too, as in
> [Looking around as a visitor](/guide/looking-around). Drafts, the commit history and the roles are shown to those who
> are signed in.

> [!DATA]
> A commit keeps who made it, with its time and comment. The history shows each version's number, time, comment,
> licence and copyright.

> [!NOTE]
> An entry that data cites is never deleted. Deprecating it removes it from the next version, while the data keeps it,
> as [Looking after the ontology](/guide/looking-after-the-ontology) shows.
