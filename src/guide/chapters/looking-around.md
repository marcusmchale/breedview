# Looking around as a visitor

Anyone can look around BreedGraph without an account. Visitors see what teams have made public: programmes, trials,
studies and their data, places, plant material and the ontology that describes the data. Everything else waits until
you sign in.

> [!WHO]
> Anyone, without an account. You only need to accept the Terms of Use.

## Before you start

Open BreedView in your browser. You need nothing else.

![Looking around as a visitor](looking-around.mp4)

## Steps

1. **The Terms of Use come first.** BreedGraph shows them before anything else. Tick "I have read and agree to the
   Terms of Use", then Accept. The privacy notice is linked from the same page: you consent to it only when you
   register an account. {#terms}
2. **Programs lists what teams have made public.** Open the Winter Wheat Breeding Programme, then its trial,
   Preliminary Yield Trial 2024, and its study, Long Meadow Yield Trial 2024. Each says whom to credit when you use
   it: the team that holds it, here the Wheat Breeding Group, Example Plant Breeding Institute. {#programmes}
3. **A study's Datasets say what was recorded.** Each variable and factor is listed with its number of records and
   the dates they cover. {#datasets}
4. **View datasets shows the records themselves.** Choose the variables and the factor. The study's licence must be
   accepted before the data loads: tick "I have read and agree to this licence", Accept, then Load Table.
   [Exploring a study's data](/guide/exploring-data) shows what more the table can do. {#view}
5. **Locations shows the farm and its fields.** Choose the farm to see its fields on the map. The maps come from
   BreedGraph's own server, so your browser contacts no one else. Places a team keeps private are not shown.
   {#locations}
6. **The map's search finds places.** Search for one by name, such as Tullamore, the county town: locations in
   BreedGraph are listed first, then GeoNames' places, in the chosen location's country, this view or anywhere, each
   with what it is and where. Choosing one shows it on the map. BreedGraph's own server answers the search, so it goes
   to no one else. {#search}
7. **Units shows what is at each place.** A unit graph links the levels at which an experiment is managed and
   observed: here, the 2024 trial area holds its plots. Each unit inherits its location, germplasm and factors from
   the units above it. {#units}
8. **Germplasm shows how the lines were bred.** Search for a line, then drag it clear of its neighbours to see its
   parents and offspring. {#germplasm}
9. **The ontology says what each variable means.** Search for a variable and drag it clear to see the trait, method
   and scale it links to. Its card gives its description. Its contributors are credited together, and those who chose
   to be are named: here, Dara Data. {#ontology}
10. **Sign in to see more.** People, private studies and who controls each record are shown only to users who are
    signed in. {#log-in}

## Who sees what

> [!SEES]
> A visitor sees only what its team released publicly. In the demonstration, that means the 2024 programme, trial,
> study and data, the farm and its fields, the 2024 trial area and its plots, the public varieties and lines, and the
> published ontology. Records released to registered users or kept private are not shown, and nor are Persons or the
> controllers of records.

> [!DATA]
> No personal data is shown to visitors: Persons, who contributed to each dataset and who controls each record are
> shown only to users who are signed in. Accepting the Terms of Use, or a study's licence, is kept in a cookie in your
> browser, for 7 days if you are not signed in. BreedGraph keeps no record of who accepted, nor of what anyone searches
> for on the map.
