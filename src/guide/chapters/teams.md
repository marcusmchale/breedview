# Joining a team, and what access means

Every record in BreedGraph is controlled by a team, and released privately, to registered users or to the public. An
affiliation with a team gives you access to its records: Read to see those it keeps private, Write to add records for
it, Curate to change them, and Admin to manage the team and who is in it.

> [!WHO]
> Anyone signed in can ask a team for access. The team's administrators decide, and how far the access reaches.

## Before you start

You need an account: see [Joining BreedGraph](/guide/joining). Here Nina, who joined the Wheat Breeding Group by
invitation, asks the Example Plant Breeding Institute for Read access, and Dara, who administers the Institute, decides.

![Joining a team, and what access means](teams.mp4)

## Steps

1. **Access control lists the teams you can ask.** You see each team tree's root, and the teams you belong to below
   it, with the access you hold: R, W, C and A for Read, Write, Curate and Admin. The teams you are not in stay out of
   sight. {#teams}
2. **Request Affiliation asks a team for access.** Nina asks the Institute, the root of her team's tree, for Read.
   {#request}
3. **The team's administrators decide.** Dara is told by email, and opens the Institute's Manage Affiliations. She
   approves Nina's request as heritable, which gives Read on the teams within the Institute too. {#approve}
4. **Nina now sees the Institute's private records.** The study Grain Quality 2025, which the Grain Quality Lab keeps
   private, is listed for her. {#sees}
5. **A record's release says who else can read it.** Private: only the teams with access. Registered: anyone signed
   in. Public: anyone, without an account. {#releases}

## Who sees what

> [!SEES]
> A request is listed for the administrators of the team asked, until they decide it. A team's private records are
> seen by those with Read access to it, directly or as heritable from a team above it; records released to registered
> users or the public, more widely.

> [!DATA]
> A request records who asked, for which team and access, and when. The administrators see your full name and
> username with it, and are emailed about it.
